FAQ

Common questions

Is the calculator really free?

Yes. It runs in your browser, needs no sign-up, and nothing you type is saved or sent to us.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's general cashflow planning help. We don't recommend financial products or give tax or investment advice.

What happens in a cash session?

We fill in a simple 13-week cash sheet together from your own figures: money in, money out, and any tax bills you already know are coming. The sheet shows how your runway changes when a figure changes. We don't tell you what to do with your money.

What do sessions cover?

Sessions are general cashflow planning only. We fill in a 13-week cash sheet from your own figures and show how your runway changes if a figure changes. We don’t advise on which debts or credit agreements to pay first, how to deal with lenders or creditors, or on loans, credit cards, overdrafts, investments, pensions, insurance or tax. If you’re a sole trader (or in a partnership) and want help prioritising personal or business debts or credit agreements, please contact Business Debtline (businessdebtline.org, 0800 197 6026), a free debt advice charity authorised by the FCA. Debt advice to individuals, including sole traders, is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and RunwayCheck is not authorised to give it. If you’re a company director worried the company can’t pay its debts, speak to an accountant or a licensed insolvency practitioner promptly.

What if my runway is short?

Then the calculator shows that early. In a session, the sheet shows how the months change if money coming in (for example invoicing and chasing), or costs you control, change — without judgement. We don’t advise on which debts to pay first; if you’re a sole trader and want help with debts, Business Debtline (businessdebtline.org) gives free, FCA-regulated advice. For free money guidance, MoneyHelper (moneyhelper.org.uk) can help, and StepChange (stepchange.org) offers free help with personal debt.

Do I need my accounts ready?

No. A recent bank balance and a rough idea of monthly income and costs is enough to start.
